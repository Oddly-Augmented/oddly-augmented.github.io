## I've tried to learn Blender more times than I can count.

The pattern is usually the same: I find a video or tutorial I like, I follow along for a bit… and then I stop. I’ll make half a donut or a basic scene, and once the video is over or something else demands my attention I close Blender and don’t open it again for weeks. I’ve never stuck around long enough to actually understand what I’m doing. It feels like I’m just copying someone else’s work instead of learning how to make my own stuff.

This is what people mean when they talk about “tutorial hell,” and I’ve been stuck there for a while.

### Life

Part of the problem with learning a new hobby is life. I’m juggling classes, work, doctor’s appointments, and a bunch of other projects, so it’s easy for Blender to fall to the bottom of the list. When I’m tired, watching someone else’s animated short or a scene made with Blender makes me want to create something too. The sad part is that I know I could make cool stuff; I just need to dedicate the time to learn the tools.

Every time I bounce off Blender, the desire to actually learn it doesn’t go away. I still want to be able to open it up and build something from scratch, not just follow another donut video.

### Finding a way

What I’m realizing is that I don’t have to “fix” this in one big push. The only way out of tutorial hell is the slow way: keep showing up, bit by bit.

That means a few things for me:
- Making a set schedule for learning: an hour or two a day.
- Experimenting more: if I watch a tutorial, I try to pause and experiment instead of just copying everything blindly.
- Remembering that practice makes progress: finishing a project even if it isn’t perfect, and taking notes on the parts I liked and didn’t like.
- Accepting that it’s fine to learn Blender in tiny slices of time between everything else, as long as I keep coming back.

Life is always going to get in the way of learning something new. There will always be homework, shifts at work, other interests, and random distractions. But the desire to learn Blender has stuck around through all of that, and that’s worth listening to. I don’t have to be perfect or ultra-productive; I just have to keep taking small steps and showing up, even when it’s inconvenient.
